# ISR History Research Protocol

## Evidence grades

### Grade A — Primary / official

Examples:
- original ISR/RMITIS documents;
- constitutions;
- minutes;
- committee lists;
- election results;
- original social posts;
- official RMIT records;
- government records;
- parliamentary records;
- archival records;
- original correspondence.

A Grade A source proves what that source itself records.
It does not automatically make every allegation within the source true.

### Grade B — Strong independent evidence

Examples:
- peer-reviewed research;
- academic studies;
- reputable contemporary journalism;
- scholarly books;
- independent institutional research.

### Grade C — Supporting historical evidence

Examples:
- Muslim student guides;
- old directories;
- another MSA's posts;
- archived community webpages;
- republished material;
- event listings;
- historical web indexes.

### Grade D — Research lead

Examples:
- LinkedIn biographies;
- personal recollections;
- unattributed screenshots;
- isolated social posts;
- search snippets;
- oral-history leads;
- claims that currently lack provenance.

Grade D evidence is retained, not discarded.

## Every research entry should capture

- ID
- Date or period
- Event / claim
- Exact wording where important
- People
- Organisations
- Location
- Source title
- Source publisher/author
- Source URL
- Source date
- Date accessed
- Evidence grade
- What the source actually proves
- What it does NOT prove
- Conflicts with other evidence
- Follow-up action
- Public-safe status
- Notes

## Historical political activity

If ISR/RMITIS supported a public political or community statement:
record the historical participation.

Do not automatically describe that position as:
- current ISR policy;
- permanent ISR ideology;
- the position of every past or future committee.

## Sensitive allegations

Harassment, assault, discrimination and misconduct allegations must
remain explicitly attributed unless an authoritative finding exists.

Example:

GOOD:
"ABC reported that a student said..."

BAD:
"RMIT caused..."

unless the latter proposition is independently established.

## Founding-date rule

Prayer infrastructure date != Society founding date.

Approximate early-1990s prayer-room evidence may support the history
of Muslim life at RMIT, but it cannot by itself establish when the
Islamic Society was legally or organisationally founded.

## Conflicts

Never silently choose one version.

Create a conflict record and retain:
- source A;
- source B;
- likely explanations;
- what evidence would resolve the issue.

## Dead websites

Dead URLs are evidence.

Preserve:
- exact URL;
- page title;
- date referenced;
- source that cited it;
- Wayback/PANDORA target;
- known filename/query string.

## Oral history

Potential witnesses should be recorded even before interview.

Oral testimony should later record:
- interviewee;
- their connection to ISR;
- period involved;
- interview date;
- consent status;
- recording/transcript location;
- statements requiring documentary corroboration.
